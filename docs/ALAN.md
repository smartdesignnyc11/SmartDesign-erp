# How Alan thinks

A working profile of Alan Mishali, founder of SmartDesign NYC and Mercer & Bond. It is distilled from his messages in the build sessions (2026-10-04 onward) and from his own document, `docs/STANDARD.md`. The chief of staff (`.claude/agents/chief-of-staff.md`) works from it. Alan corrects it. When he does, the change is logged in `docs/DECISIONS.md` and this file is updated the same day.

His ChatGPT history is not in here yet. When he exports it, only the patterns are added here. The raw text stays outside the repo (see "Privacy" at the end).

## What he is building, in his words
- A **custom, tailored ERP for SmartDesign, built for the future.** It replaces NetSuite, QuickBooks, Procore, Buildertrend and a CRM.
- A **living, evolving system.** A brain consumes every record, email, transcript and bank line and learns from them. Each person has an assistant that acts on their behalf.
- **Nothing slips.** No forgotten task, follow-up or vendor promise.
- **Money leaks are caught automatically:** overspending, unbilled work, unbilled reimbursables, uncredited refunds, paying more than before, buying what is already in stock.
- **Financial truth.** Every figure ties to a bank or card line. Journal entries are flagged. Nothing posted changes silently. Every transaction matches a line item. QuickBooks is only a bridge.
- **"To the highest level, especially the AI features."**

## What he values (his Standard, in one breath)
- Own it.
- The client comes first.
- Craft without compromise.
- Think ahead (three weeks).
- Every dollar matters.
- Truth, early.
- One team.

When values conflict, the order is: safety, law and landmarks, integrity, quality, client experience, schedule, cost.

On why money matters (his words): "money wasted hurts employees from getting raises, bonuses, and additional staff/resources, and takes away from the value we can give to the client at no additional cost."

## How he decides
- **Decisive and terse.** He wants a recommendation, not a menu. Ask only when the answer is genuinely his.
- **He thinks in real workflows.** His examples are concrete: 24 sheets of 5/8" drywall with 12 in stock; lumber mills and where wood is cheapest. Build from the example outward to the class of problem.
- **He gives context, not quotas.** "75% of purchases start from a line item" described how the firm works; he did not want a benchmark. Don't turn an observation into a metric, rule or KPI unless he asks for one. Use it to shape design: defaults, navigation, what links to what.
- **Autonomy with guardrails.** He wants the AI to run things on its own (follow-ups, filing, tracking, research). He also wants people to approve anything that commits money, commits the firm or reaches a client. Autonomy comes through delegation he can see and switch off.
- **Separation of duties.** The person who made a purchase does not approve their own explanation. Baseline and rule changes go to the owner.
- **Only the right person overrides.** Overrides need a reason and leave a trail.
- **Total cost, not sticker price.** This means direct versus middleman, out-of-state versus NYC, freight and lead time. Never trade quality for price without approval.
- **Integrity with vendors.** He pushes hard on price, but within the Standard, so no fake competing quotes.
- **He adds requirements in a stream.** Each new one is folded into the right lane and the spec the same hour, not queued for "later".
- **He wants me to think past his ask.** For every request, answer three questions: what is he not thinking about, how could this be better, and what goes wrong if it isn't thought through? Bring back only what needs his call.

## How he reasons (patterns from 2026-10-11; use the same reasoning)
- **From one concrete case to the whole class.** "24 sheets with 12 in stock" became inventory-first at every door. "Lumber mills" became sourcing research for every category.
- **He follows the money and the trust.** Who could be overpaid, what isn't billed, what a client could be charged wrongly, who approves whose explanation.
- **He walks the workflow end to end and asks what happens at each hand-off,** especially on the go and in the field.
- **He checks the evidence behind "it works".** "How did the experts not catch that?" A test that didn't catch it is itself a bug.
- **He wants the people who use it consulted,** and wants their real experience, not opinions about a list.
- **He asks for structure before building** (the menu: "let's discuss and have a plan"), then wants speed once it's agreed.
- **He cares how it looks to the client** as much as how it works for staff.
- **He wants autonomy with control.** Agents act on their own where delegated, people approve commitments, and he can see and switch everything.
- **He keeps raising the bar against the leaders** (NetSuite, QuickBooks, Salesforce), because this one is built for his industry.
- **He watches cost.** He chose fewer roles and shorter days when usage was high. Give him the real number and the trade-offs.

## How he works with me
- **He reads on an iPhone,** often between sites. Replies are short, lead with the answer, and use plain words with no jargon or internal IDs.
- **He is cost-conscious about usage.** Warn him before anything that uses a lot. Never buy or turn on extra usage. He did not approve extra spending, and was alarmed when he thought he had been charged.
- **He wants to see it.** He asks for a link to the live app after changes, and judges the app by how it feels on the phone: clean, organised, never overwhelming.
- **He wants honesty about status.** Say what is built and tested, what is still a spec, and what waits on the platform.
- **He tests with real accounts,** for testing only. His Gmail, Calendar and Drive are connected, and QuickBooks will be when it is re-authorised. Real data never goes into the repo.
- **He defers what he doesn't yet understand.** NY rules (§7) were explicitly left for later. Don't push them.
