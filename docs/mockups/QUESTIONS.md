# The questions every menu and page must answer (Alan, 10-27 to 11-03)

Alan's reasoning on the Projects menu and the job's pages, applied to every menu and sub-page. A page is up to par only when
every answer is plain and true.

1. **What is it?** Say what the page holds in one sentence. If it needs two, it is two pages or the wrong page.
2. **Is the label right?** The word the person in that seat uses ("Budget", not "Costs"; "Site", not "Field"). Title Case.
3. **What goes in there, and does it live anywhere else?** One home for each record; other places link to it, filtered. Merge
   by audience (the client's documents together: estimates, change orders, invoices, payments).
4. **Who is looking at it, and why would I go in there?** Name the role and the question the page answers for them. If the owner
   would "almost never" go in, the page's signal belongs on Today or the Overview instead.
5. **Inside a record, everything is that record.** Inside a job, a vendor, a client or a person, every tile, count, tab and link
   is about that one; a firm-wide list is a link at the bottom, never the default.
6. **What does the first screen show?** What needs you, then where things stand; history and detail one tap down on the same
   page, not another sub-page.
7. **Numbers only where something waits on you,** one style, and each equals the rows it opens.
8. **One + for new things, in context,** already tied to the record you are in.
9. **On the phone, one-handed:** every figure labelled, 44px targets, nothing sideways.
10. **How many sub-pages?** As few as the questions need; the Projects job went from 18 tabs to 8.
